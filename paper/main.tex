\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Adjuvant chemotherapy with levamisole and 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) after
resection of stage B/C colon carcinoma was established by the North
Central Cancer Treatment Group (NCCTG) in the late 1980s, and the
resulting individual-patient dataset has become one of the most widely
used teaching resources in survival analysis. We ask what a modern
biostatistical toolkit can extract from the same trial today. Among 929
patients randomized to observation ($n=315$), levamisole ($n=310$), or
levamisole plus 5-FU ($n=304$), we reconstruct a composite disease-free
survival (DFS) endpoint (recurrence or death; 506 events) from the
published event-type coding and integrate six families of methods in one
fully reproducible pipeline: (i) Kaplan--Meier and Cox analyses with
multiple imputation, competing-risk sensitivity analyses, and
absolute-benefit measures including the restricted mean survival time;
(ii) a redesign of the trial as a three-look group-sequential design with
O'Brien--Fleming efficacy and non-binding beta-spending futility
boundaries, re-enacted on the real event times; (iii) Monte Carlo
simulation studies of covariate-adjustment efficiency, sequential-design
operating characteristics, and confidence-interval coverage under
non-proportional hazards; (iv) cross-validated survival prediction with
five machine-learning models; (v) a Bayesian Weibull analysis with
skeptical, weakly-informative, and enthusiastic priors and sequential
monitoring; and (vi) doubly robust estimation of heterogeneous absolute
treatment effects. Levamisole plus 5-FU reduced the DFS hazard by 38\%
(adjusted HR $=0.621$, 95\% CI $0.496$--$0.776$), gained 16.8 percentage
points of five-year DFS and 0.63 disease-free years in restricted mean
survival time. Under the redesigned monitoring plan the trial would have
stopped for efficacy at the first interim analysis (118 events; HR
$0.52$, $Z=3.38$ against a boundary of $2.96$), and Bayesian monitoring
would have signalled benefit at 25\% of the information. Covariate
adjustment raised simulated power from 72.6\% to 80.9\% for a moderate
effect and directly illustrated the non-collapsibility of the hazard
ratio, while the collapsible RMST difference was unchanged by adjustment.
All code, data, and outputs are reproducible end-to-end from the
accompanying repository.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data availability}
The trial data are the \texttt{colon} dataset distributed with the R
package \texttt{survival} \citep{therneau2026}, derived from the NCCTG
adjuvant colon cancer program \citep{laurie1989,moertel1990} and obtained
from the Rdatasets mirror; they are redistributed with the repository in
\texttt{data/raw/colon.csv} with full attribution for research and
educational use.

\section*{Code availability}
All analysis code (six R modules and four Python modules), the
reproducibility environment files, and every figure and table in this
paper are generated from the accompanying repository
(\texttt{Makefile}: \texttt{make all}), with fixed random seeds
(global seed 20260916) and module-to-module communication exclusively
through versioned files in \texttt{data/} and \texttt{outputs/}.
Exact software versions are recorded in
\texttt{environment/r\_session\_info.txt} and
\texttt{environment/python\_package\_versions.txt}.
